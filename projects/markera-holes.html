The detector

A box around every hole

The first step of every scan is a YOLO11 object detector trained on a single class, the bullet hole. It looks at the photo at 1536 × 1536 pixels and returns a bounding box and a confidence for each hole it sees. The app keeps the boxes at 45 percent confidence or more.

The model is exported to ONNX, so one file runs on both Android and iOS, on the phone's CPU. The box carries more than a position: its size is the hole's size, and scoring uses it to gauge by the hole's edge, so a shot whose edge breaks a line counts as the higher ring, as it does on paper.

The start

74 hand-labelled photos

The first detector, in June 2026, was a YOLOv8 model trained on 74 photos with every hole marked by hand. It worked early and stayed the first step of the pipeline while the geometry around it was rebuilt several times, which the second part describes.

The training data

Approve series for training

Every scan that syncs to the backend can become training data. Photos and hole positions are anonymized, and each series is checked by hand before it joins the training set; the app's privacy page tells users that saved photos and holes help improve hole detection.

A review page serves a random unreviewed series beside the verdicts so far: A approves it, R rejects it, and holes can be fixed on a training copy while the user's own series stays as they left it. The training export then zips the approved series only.

The admin

Every series on its photo

Each series opens on its photo with the detected holes, the four rim probes and the centre drawn on it, beside a table of every hole's position, detected and manual score and distance. Scores, holes and the rim can be corrected in place; every edit saves at once and lands in a tracked change history.

The training

A sharper detector from approved series

Since November the hole detector learns from the series shooters save in the app. Three batches of approved series grew the set from the original 74 hand-labelled photos to 574 photos with 2933 holes, each model trained as YOLO11m on one class, the hole, at 1536 px.

On the same 96 held-out photos with 548 holes, at the app's 45 percent confidence threshold, the latest model finds 529 holes where the June model found 497, and gets 78 photos exactly right — every hole found and nothing extra — where the June model got 55.

Box fit matters because Markera reads a hole's size from its box to judge edge hits. It rose from 0.573 to 0.673 mAP50-95 for the trained model, and the half-precision ONNX file the app runs on the phone keeps that lead over the model it replaced.

The problem

Two parts: the holes, and the rings they land in

Scoring a series from a photo takes two things. The app has to find every bullet hole, and it has to find the centre and the rings of the target, seen at whatever angle the phone was held, to measure each hole against. This page sums up both.

Part 1 · summary

Finding the holes

A YOLO11 model, exported to ONNX so the same file runs on Android and iOS, draws a bounding box around every bullet hole, and the size of each box tells the scoring how far the hole's edge reaches.

Since November the detector learns from series that shooters save in the app, checked by hand on an admin page. The training set grew from 74 hand-labelled photos to 574, and on the same held-out photos the detector now finds 529 of 548 holes, up from 497.

Part 2 · summary

Finding the centre and the rings

A hole 40 mm from the centre is a 9 or an 8 depending on where the centre is and how much the camera tilted, and a paper target is covered in look-alike circles. Markera lets the printed ring digits decide: on-device OCR reads them, and lines through the digit rows cross at the true centre.

Four probe disks then start where the digits predict the black 6/7 ring and walk to its rim. Those four points pin down the full perspective map from photo to paper, and every hole goes through it for edge-gauged scoring. On a real phone every probe reached the rim in 40 of 42 scans. A last fit to the white lines inside the black then cuts the centre error from 0.80 to 0.20 mm against hand-labelled photos. Seven approaches were tried to get there.

The result

A scored series from one photo

Together the two parts score the series on the spot. The five best hits pre-fill the pickers and stay editable, because the shooter is the final authority on their own series. When the ring fit looks implausible, the app leaves the scoring to the shooter.

The app

History and statistics

Each series is tagged with a caliber, from a shared list of 24 that also scales the hit-dot overlay, and an optional free-text label. Series sync to its own backend and build a local history grouped by day and filterable by caliber, tag and date range, with zip export.

A statistics screen plots filtered groups on a zoomable drawn target with mean and median impact markers, or charts any measure over time with trend, mean and ±1 SD lines. An Insights tab turns that history into coaching: eight finding types — offset, stretched groups, fliers, wide or low days, trend and session fatigue — each carrying a confidence and a plain-language cause, surfaced on Statistics, as a Home card for the strongest recent one, and as a sight helper that turns a stable offset into a click correction. Settings add a language switch, light/dark theme and an insights on/off switch. One sight adjustment between two .22 competitions, read off those markers (in Swedish).

The problem

Which ring is it, really?

A YOLOv8 model finds the bullet holes, and that part worked early. The hard problem was geometry: a hole 40 mm from the centre is a 9 or an 8 depending on where the centre actually is and how much the camera tilted — and a paper target is covered in look-alike circles.

The approach

Let the printed digits decide

On-device OCR (ML Kit on Android, Apple Vision on iOS) reads the printed ring digits; lines through the horizontal and vertical rows cross at the true centre. Four probe disks then walk from where the digits predict the 6/7 ring to the actual black-to-white rim, and an ellipse through them undoes the perspective for edge-gauged scoring.

Seven approaches were tried; three pieces survived. How that detection was arrived at, with screenshots of every approach that failed first.

The result

40 of 42 scans on the rim

In the first run on a real phone every probe landed on the rim in 40 of 42 scans. The five best hits pre-fill the pickers and stay editable, because the shooter is the final authority on their own series.

The app

History and statistics

Each series is tagged with a caliber, from a shared list of 24 that also scales the hit-dot overlay, and an optional free-text label. Series sync to its own backend and build a local history grouped by day and filterable by caliber, tag and date range, with zip export.

A statistics screen plots filtered groups on a zoomable drawn target with mean and median impact markers, or charts any measure over time with trend, mean and ±1 SD lines. Settings add a language switch and light/dark theme.
